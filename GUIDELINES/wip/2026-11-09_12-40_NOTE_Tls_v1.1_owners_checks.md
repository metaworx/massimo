# NOTE Tls v1.1: owners checks

The owner's day that Blocks 2, 4 and 5 of
[AP Tls v1.1](2026-11-06_12-42_AP_Tls_serve_tls.md) left open
([its run's NOTE](2026-11-06_14-05_NOTE_Tls_v1.1_how_the_run_went.md), item 10),
run on 2026-11-08 and 2026-11-09 on the owner's signals;
nothing was sent over the radio, and every box was put back as it was.
Names and addresses are placeholders here.

1. **Check 1, Let's Encrypt by a port (Block 4).**
   No box on the owner's LAN has a public address,
   and both port challenges need one,
   so the check ran against Pebble v2.10.1 and its DNS test server on 127.0.0.1,
   with `serve` from `main` at `680631c` (the owner's choice).
   - TLS-ALPN-01 on the listener: valid at once, the certificate 3 s later,
     served, `/healthz` reporting it.
   - HTTP-01 with `-tls-http`: Pebble's TLS port pointed at a closed one,
     so TLS-ALPN-01 failed and autocert fell back to HTTP-01 by itself;
     the certificate 3 s later.
   - autocert answers a challenge only for a `Host` on its list,
     which has no port:
     Pebble's checker, aimed at a port other than 80, sends one,
     and an unknown token gets 403 with it, 404 without.
     A relay standing in for the router's forward stripped it.
     Let's Encrypt always comes in on port 80, so nothing changes.
   - The plain port redirects to `https://<name>:443/` whatever `-listen` says
     (autocert's fallback): right behind a router that forwards 443,
     wrong on the LAN when the listener is on another port.
   - Nothing in the log says that autocert brought a certificate;
     the DNS-01 source logs one line when it has one.
2. **Check 2, DNS-01 through a hook (Block 5).**
   A Raspberry Pi 5 with the Debian package 0.12.0~alpha.1,
   its name in a zone at Cloudflare pointing at its LAN address:
   - Configured as `packaging/README.md` says:
     `cloudflare.sh` from `main` under `/usr/local/lib/massimo/`,
     the token file, `massimo install -tls acme -tls-names massimo.example.net
     -acme-dns-hook … -listen :8443`.
     0.12.0's `install` has no `-acme-directory` yet (pull request #116 adds it),
     so the staging directory's line was written into the settings first,
     which that `install` keeps on a switch to `acme`.
   - The token was the one the box's own acme.sh keeps for its other certificates,
     copied for the check and shredded after;
     the hook found the zone by walking up the name, without a zone ID.
   - The hook's `set` ran 3 s after the start, the certificate was issued
     14 s after it (issuer "(STAGING) …"), and the record was gone from the
     zone's name server afterwards.
     The log shows no `clear`: the server logs a hook run only when the hook
     prints something, and a `clear` that works prints nothing
     (one that fails is a warning).
   - The router's DNS answers nothing for a name that points at a LAN address
     (its rebind protection, measured by the owner),
     so the box had 1.1.1.1 as its first resolver for the check.
   - `jq` was missing on the box, and the package names neither `jq` nor `curl`;
     `install` warned "no MASSIMO_API_TOKEN: the API is open to the LAN"
     while the server ran with `auth=users`.
     Both are plans of their own (`HookDeps`, `AuthWarning`).
3. **The Infomaniak hook against the real API (Block 5)**,
   on 2026-11-08 on another box: `set` and `clear`,
   the record on both name servers within 10 s and gone within 10 s.
4. **Check 3, the self-signed notice on a phone (Block 2).**
   An iPhone with Safari at `https://192.0.2.10:8443/`, on 2026-11-09:
   - The warning can be passed, and the certificate view shows the box's name
     as subject and issuer and the end of its validity.
   - The certificate is valid 825 days and an hour (`NotBefore` an hour back,
     `NotAfter` 825 days from now);
     Safari did not mind,
     since Apple's limit applies to a certificate it trusts.
   - The sign-in dialog is modal and covers the header's notice:
     before signing in only its first words show, the fingerprint not at all.
   - The fingerprint the page shows proves nothing in any case:
     it comes over the very connection it is meant to check,
     and a man in the middle passes the server's along, or rewrites it.
     The browser's certificate view against `massimo tls show` on the box
     is the comparison that holds.
5. **Check 4, the reverse proxy (Block 3)**,
   from another machine through nginx on the same Pi on 2026-11-08:
   the event stream unbuffered (state events after 0.04 to 0.10 s,
   the keep-alive at 30.04 s) and the client's address taken from the forwarded headers.
